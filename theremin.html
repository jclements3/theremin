The build at a glance

The D-Lev is a digital theremin built around an off-the-shelf WaveShare CoreEP4CE6 FPGA board plus five custom through-hole PCBs, a 3D-printed enclosure, and hand-wound antenna coils. Licensed MIT. A competent hobbyist with soldering + FPGA experience has a complete path from these files to a working instrument — see the Assembly tab for the order of operations.

Decisions locked so far: full room (~20 × 14 ft), lean budget (~$800–1,500), 3D-printed enclosure, and a walk-out pasture door on the left wall used for fume venting.

Why it's laid out this way

  • Electronics bench on the top wall — outlets are everywhere, so placement follows workflow; solder/fume end sits by the pasture door to vent outdoors.
  • Calibration zone far right — antennas are capacitive sensors, so keep ~6–8 ft of clear floor away from metal, shelving, and the pasture-door draft.
  • Center island (storage, coil bench, printer) keeps the walk lane between the two doors clear.
Pasture door = dirt, pollen, draft. Add a boot mat, cover the scope/printer when idle, keep the printer out of the draft line, and close the door during final calibration.

Assembly — order of operations

  1. Fab the PCBs. Upload the ready gerbers in JLCPCB/ (one zip per board), or open the KiCad projects to tweak first.~1–2 weeks lead time · do this first
  2. Order parts. Work the full BOM (D-Lev_kit_parts.ods) into supplier carts. Add IC sockets so you never desolder a chip.order alongside the PCBs
  3. Solder the boards. Through-hole: main, AFE, encoders, tuner. Sockets first, low-profile parts, then tall ones. Inspect with the magnifier.station: electronics bench
  4. Flash the FPGA. Quartus Lite + USB-Blaster. Load output_file.jic as-is, or recompile from d-lev_fpga_source. Hive images: .mif/.spi.no compile needed to start
  5. Print the enclosure. OpenSCAD .scad → STL → slice → print in PETG. Keep the printer out of the door draft.station: 3D printer · runs while you solder
  6. Wind & tune the antenna coils. Use the tcoil software (Research section) to target inductance; verify with the LCR meter.station: coil-wind bench · the fiddly part
  7. Assemble. Mount boards + FPGA in the printed case, fit antennas, wire it up.station: mech-assembly bench
  8. Calibrate. In the open zone, run the antenna pitch/volume cal from the User Manual. Close the pasture door first.station: calibration zone
  9. Play-test & dial in presets. Use the manual's preset/librarian workflow.done — make music
Steps 1 + 2 have the longest lead times and gate everything else — kick them off before buying a single tool if you want to overlap shipping with shop setup.

Build schedule

Sequenced for a part-time cadence: ~3 weeknights (×2.5 h) + Saturday (~6 h) ≈ 12–14 h/week, finishing in roughly 3 months. The key move is firing off the long-lead orders in week 1 so the PCBs and parts ship while you build out the shop — the two tracks overlap instead of stacking.

Also saved standalone as diagrams/d-lev_build_schedule.svg.

Cadence math & assumptions

  • ~12–14 h/week = three weeknights at ~2.5 h plus a ~6 h Saturday. Two slow weeks ≈ one of these.
  • Weeks 1–3 are mostly waiting on fab + shipping — spend that bench-free time on the shop.
  • Soldering (wks 5–8) is the long pole on the build side; printing overlaps it since the printer runs unattended.
  • Coil winding + calibration are the risk — that's why there's a dedicated buffer band before "first sound."
Holidays, a tricky solder joint, or a coil that won't tune will stretch this. Treat the 14 weeks as working weeks, not calendar weeks. Want it re-paced — fewer hours, a hard deadline, or a 2-instrument run? Drop it in the notes.
